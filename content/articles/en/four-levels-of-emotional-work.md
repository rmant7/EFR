Most people work with emotions the same way they work with a system that starts to lag.

The mechanism that produces emotional reactions is rarely examined. Only the output is managed — "I got triggered."

In simplified form, there are four levels of dealing with emotional states.

**Level 0 — Ignoring system failures**

The system freezes from time to time, but this is considered normal.

In emotional terms: irritation, anxiety, tension, sudden reactions are treated as a natural background.

There is no separation between the state and the self.

**Level 1 — Restarting the reaction**

When the system freezes, it is restarted.

In emotional terms: holding back, staying silent, distracting oneself, "not reacting," waiting it out.

Only behavior is managed.

The same states return in similar situations.

**Level 2 — Symptom optimization**

The system becomes more stable.

In emotional terms: self-regulation practices, psychology, coaching, various forms of inner work.

Reactions become weaker, shorter, more manageable.

But the underlying mechanism that generates them remains unchanged.

**Level 3 — Removing the source of failure**

The engineer does not optimize behavior. The bug that creates the freeze is removed.

In emotional terms, this points to a different type of observation: not managing or suppressing reactions, but the disappearance of the repetitive triggering mechanism itself.

**The key shift:**

Most approaches work with what has already appeared.

Very few examine what continuously produces its reappearance.

Emotional states in this model are not random events, but recurring patterns of system operation.

The question shifts from "how do I deal with this" to "what keeps generating this again and again."
