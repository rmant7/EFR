Once decisions scale beyond your own work, a pattern doesn't just cost you time — it costs your team's, multiplied by however many people are waiting on you.

A few patterns that show up often at this level:

**Conflict avoidance** — a difficult conversation happens weeks too late, after the cost of avoiding it has already compounded across the team.

**Need for approval** — decisions wait for a consensus that was never actually required, and the delay becomes the real cost.

**Perfectionism** — initiatives ship months later than they should, and the team quietly learns to expect that as normal.

**Defensiveness** — feedback gets set aside until it becomes a crisis nobody can avoid discussing anymore.

These patterns rarely announce themselves as emotional. They look like a management style, a way of working, "just how things are done here." EFR isn't another leadership framework to apply in the moment — it works with the pattern underneath, so the reaction it used to produce stops repeating.
