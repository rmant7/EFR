Founders make an outsized number of decisions every day, often alone, often with incomplete information. That's exactly the condition where a recurring emotional pattern has the most leverage — not because founders are more emotional than anyone else, but because there's less structure around them to absorb it.

A few patterns that show up often at this stage:

**Fear of failure** — safe, incremental bets quietly replace the asymmetric ones that actually move an early company forward.

**Need for control** — hiring stalls, or the people you do hire stop being used for what they're good at, because delegating still feels riskier than doing it yourself.

**Scarcity mindset** — real investment gets postponed in the moments that would have compounded the most.

**Overthinking** — the fast-moving opportunity closes while you're still building the case for it.

None of these show up labeled as an emotion. They show up as a decision, a delay, a hire that didn't happen. EFR doesn't ask you to be more disciplined about catching yourself in the moment — it works with the pattern that's generating the hesitation or the overreach before the decision is made.
